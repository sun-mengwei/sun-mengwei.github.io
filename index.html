About me

I am a fourth-year PhD student in applied mathematics at the Georgia Institute of Technology. I am very fortunate to be advised by Prof. Haomin Zhou. Before joining Georgia Tech, I earned a bachelor’s degree in mathematics and statistics from the University of Wisconsin–Madison.

My research interests include optimal transport and Wasserstein geometry, dynamical systems and numerical analysis, stochastic filtering, and scientific machine learning. I am interested in developing interpretable, learning-based numerical methods for high-dimensional PDEs, dynamical systems, and the evolution of probability distributions.

Research

I am interested in applying learning-based methods to solve dynamical systems.

Deep Tangent Bundle Method for Game Dynamics

This project investigates how the tangent bundles of neural networks can be used to approximate particle dynamics in games. The method offers two advantages: it provides a global solution map rather than only local particle evaluations, and it estimates both function values and density values at arbitrary sample points.
